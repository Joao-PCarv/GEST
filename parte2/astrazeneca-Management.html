People management at AstraZeneca

Group assignment (Gestão), ISEP · HR section

AstraZeneca manages its people around inclusion and continuous learning [1], and it replaced annual rated appraisals with regular coaching conversations [2].

Human resources policy

AstraZeneca's HR policy starts from a strategic priority: to be a great place to work, which covers inclusion and diversity, lifelong learning, and ethics and transparency [1]. The scale is large: 96,100 employees at the end of 2025 (94,300 in 2024), and 86% say they believe the company is a great place to work [3].

The main principles are:

  • Merit and equal opportunity. The Code of Ethics states that hiring, pay, training and promotion depend on each person's skills, experience, conduct, performance and potential. Harassment is unacceptable and can be reported to a manager, HR or another leader, who arranges an impartial investigation [4].
  • Inclusion before diversity. The company says it first builds a culture of inclusion and belonging, so that it can then attract and retain a diverse workforce. It has a global standard for inclusion and diversity and an ambassador group sponsored by the CEO. The Board (twice a year) and the senior executive team (every quarter) review the composition of the workforce [5].
  • Diversity results. In 2025, 51.5% of senior middle management roles and above were held by women, and 87% of employees felt they could be themselves at work [3].
  • Human rights and responsible employment. The company supports the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, meets national wage and working-hours requirements as a minimum, supports International Labour Organization standards on child labour, and applies a "no recruitment fee" policy. In 2025 it launched a global standard on human rights [6].
  • Dialogue with workers. According to the 2022 survey, 45% of the countries where it operates have a relationship with trade unions, and 95% of the rest have other ways of engaging with employees [7].

Recruitment and selection

In 2025 AstraZeneca received 1.2 million applications and hired 19,000 people, of whom 7,000 (about 37%) were internal and 12,000 external [3].

Early-talent programmes. The company uses structured programmes to attract recent graduates:

  • The R&D graduate programme is a two-year scheme launched within the Innovative Medicines and Early Development unit, after the company found that its science workforce was ageing because it had recruited few young scientists during the recession. Graduates complete three eight-month rotations across research areas and are recruited to research sites in the UK, Sweden and the US [8].
  • In the UK there are also apprenticeships, whose application window normally opens in January [9].

Selection stages. According to the application-preparation website WikiJob, the process for general vacancies and graduate programmes typically comprises an online application form (personal details, CV and competency questions), a telephone interview for shortlisted candidates and an assessment centre with aptitude tests (numerical, verbal and logical reasoning), a presentation and individual or group exercises. The same site says the process can take several months [10].

Who recruits. The company's own job postings show a Talent Acquisition team dedicated to early-talent programmes. A posting for a Talent Acquisition Partner describes implementing talent acquisition strategies and overseeing recruitment into early-talent programmes, launching social media campaigns together with the Recruitment Marketing and Employer Branding partners, and using the applicant tracking system (Workday) to generate insights [11]. A posting for a coordinator adds organising the invitations and scheduling of assessment centres, posting early-talent adverts on university careers services and student portals such as Handshake, and using the applicant tracking system to produce data-rich reports [12].

The Portuguese case: the Life Changers Trainee Programme. The programme, based in Barcarena (Oeiras), selects by profile rather than degree: it looks for curious, proactive people with critical thinking, from any academic area [13].

Onboarding programmes

AstraZeneca has a global role responsible for the candidate experience and onboarding strategy [15]. In an interview, a member of the HR team describes a global onboarding project that focused on simplifying onboarding and making it consistent globally, working with 77 countries [16].

In practice, a job posting from August 2025 for an onboarding specialist in Barcelona lists the following onboarding activities [17]:

  • Before day 1: right-to-work checks, data entry in Workday, set-up of email and IT equipment, and a briefing for the hiring manager [17].
  • On day 1: a welcome session and a new-employee orientation, with senior leaders presenting the company, and a welcome box [17].

The same posting provides for quarterly sessions to collect feedback and improve the content and format of the orientation [17].

Programmes for early-career entrants. Apprentices are given a comprehensive induction plan covering both AstraZeneca and the education provider [9]. In Portugal, Life Changers trainees spend 11 months placed in a specific area and function, supported by a tutor, with career Power Sessions with leaders, reverse mentoring with the leadership team, impact projects and participation in sustainability and diversity teams [1].

Training and development

AstraZeneca uses the "3Es" learning framework (Education, Exposure and Experience), which recognises that people learn in different ways, and aims to empower employees to prioritise their own development [18]. Its approach to learning and development has four goals: Perform, Grow, Adapt and Belong [18].

The main tools are:

  • Degreed platform. The company reported over 1.96 million Degreed learning completions in the previous year, enabling over 2.1 million hours of learning. In a global pulse survey, 88% of employees reported that, in the past 12 months, they had improved their existing skills, learnt new skills or had a development opportunity [19].
  • Artificial intelligence. The Thriving in the Age of AI programme had more than 37,700 employees participating in 2025, and over 1,500 agents were developed with the AI Agent Builder in its first two months [19].
  • Languages. The Foreign Language Skills programme offers more than 70 languages [19].
  • Leadership and coaching. The company runs Manager as Coach and Leader as Coach programmes, workshops and masterclasses, peer coaching forums, AI feedforward simulations and professional coaching support, and thousands of employees received one-to-one coaching [2]. In 2025, more than 5,800 employees took part in a development programme [3].
  • Entry programmes. Trainees in Portugal take specialised courses and have a tutor, Power Sessions and reverse mentoring [1].

Interviews and press. In an interview with HR Grapevine, the VP of Global Talent & Development says that over five years more than 50,000 employees have had some development experience, with more than 4 million modules completed, and that 83% agree they have opportunities for development and growth. Employees can nominate themselves for programmes, and the Empowerment: Diversity by Design programme supports under-represented talent. The generative AI accreditation had more than 8,000 participants and 5,000 certificates issued, and 94% of learners say it is relevant to their work [20]. In Canada, a sponsored (non-editorial) feature also mentions stretch assignments, tuition reimbursement and university partnerships, and reports that more than 90% of employees learned a new skill in the past year [21].

Performance appraisal

AstraZeneca moved away from "backward looking" annual reviews and 1-to-5 performance ratings. The vision was set by the global Talent & Development team in 2020, after a May 2020 survey of 82,000 employees showed that the existing performance management approach was outdated [2].

The current model, called performance development, has these features:

  • Regular check-ins between managers and team members, including quarterly check-ins [2].
  • A dedicated coaching and feedforward capability: the model is led by a global Coaching & Feedforward capability lead and uses AI feedforward simulations for practice [2].
  • Coaching as a management skill: the Manager as Coach and Leader as Coach programmes, workshops and masterclasses, peer coaching forums and professional coaching support [2].
  • No numerical ratings: managers record qualitative notes in the system instead of scores [22].

Results published by the company itself [2]:

  • Around 13,000 managers attended a virtual practice workshop.
  • A 45% shift in employees' positive mindset towards performance development.
  • A 70% increase in managers' confidence in holding meaningful coaching conversations.
  • A 12% increase in managers' and leaders' core coaching capabilities, as measured by team members and peers.

The International Coaching Federation recognised the company as a winner of the Coaching Impact Awards 2023 [2].

What interviews and the press say. In an interview with HR Grapevine, the SVP of Reward, Inclusion and Talent Acquisition explains that the old model used 1-to-5 ratings that fed into pay decisions. Managers now record qualitative notes in the system instead of scores, hold quarterly check-ins and use International Coaching Federation coaching competencies. According to the same interview, 85% of employees receive regular coaching and more than 14,000 managers have taken part in workshops [22]. Personnel Today gave the initiative an award in 2022 (developed with LHH and Advantexe) and noted that managers learned the approach through videos, webinars and an AI simulation tool [23]. In 2023, HR Executive reported that the company was training managers and leaders in coaching in response to employees' own demand [24].

Interview excerpts

The excerpts below reproduce short verbatim answers from published interviews with AstraZeneca HR leaders. They are not full transcripts: the complete texts are on the HR Grapevine pages cited, and one of them is cut off by a subscription prompt.

Marc Howells, VP of Global Talent & Development (HR Grapevine) [20]

  • Q: 83% of AstraZeneca employees agree that they have the opportunity for development and growth. How did you reach this level? A: "We focus on creating everyday development experiences that empower our people to continuously build skills" [20].
  • Q: What is the "3Es" framework? A: "We use the '3Es' learning framework of education, exposure, and experience" [20].
  • Q: How is that approach helping upskill your workforce as we hurtle into the AI era? A: "Our generative AI accreditation programme is a key initiative in this effort" [20].

Rebekah Martin, SVP of Reward, Inclusion and Talent Acquisition (HR Grapevine) [22]

  • Q: What prompted AstraZeneca to remove once-yearly reviews and numerical ratings? A: "Employees were really dissatisfied with it. Managers didn't like it either" [22].
  • Q: What type of performance management model did you want to create instead? A: "As a result, we don't assign numbers to people anymore" [22].
  • Q: How did you get leaders, managers and employees bought into the new model? A: "It wasn't an HR initiative, it was led by the business" [22].
  • Q: How else are you developing managers' coaching skills? A: "We've also had over 14,000 line managers participating in performance development workshops" [22].

Maggie Spong, Head / VP of Talent Acquisition (HR Grapevine, 2018) [25], [26]

  • Q: With the recruitment landscape always changing, is it possible for HR to be anything more than reactive? A: "Now we are transforming into a more strategic function with a design to deliver strong candidate pipelines" [25].
  • Q: Why is recruitment an important part of talent management? A: "The recruitment process is the first step in managing your new talent" [26].
