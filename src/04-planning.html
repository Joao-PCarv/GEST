Analysis · interactions and trends

Factors interact. US tariffs, the pricing agreement and local manufacturing form one package: a three-year tariff exemption {AR|p. 4}, prices aligned with other wealthy countries {AR|p. 32}, and substantially all medicines sold in the US made in the US {AR|p. 33}. In 2026 the IRA price cap on Farxiga coincides with the loss of US exclusivity, yet the AR expects the impact to be manageable {AR|p. 33}. Ageing raises demand {AR|p. 6} while tight budgets cap prices {AR|p. 7}, which the Group answers with tiered pricing {AR|p. 41}. Geopolitics, climate and trade raise supply-chain risk, which dual sourcing and local capacity mitigate {AR|pp. 7, 44, 48}.

Medium-term trends: healthcare spending grows 7.1% a year to 2029 {AR|p. 6}, Calquence enters price negotiation in 2027 {AR|p. 33}, and the Group aims for net zero by 2045 {AR|p. 42}. Demand pressure is favourable and price and regulatory pressure is unfavourable. Political factors are ambivalent: geopolitics raises risk but rewards local investment.

Analysis

The sector is attractive for incumbents because entry barriers are very high, but the profitability of each product is temporary. While protected, buyer power weighs most; after the patent expires, generics weigh most. Success therefore depends on continuously renewing the portfolio. Two ratings need care: if generics were counted as new entrants, that force would rise; and the “weak” entry threat depends on intellectual property staying protected {AR|p. 49}.

Analysis

The resources that pass all four tests are the ability to innovate (pipeline and R&D, leadership in oncology) and, only while they last, patents. Most important internal and external aspects

The points below are ranked by weight in revenue, time horizon (nearest first), whether the AR treats them as a priority or Principal Risk, and whether they can be quantified.

Analysis

Internal situation.

  1. Revenue concentration (W1, with S2 as its opposite face): the five largest products are about 51% of revenue, Farxiga alone is 14.5% and oncology is 43.6% {AR|pp. 2, 56}.
  2. The innovation engine (S1): $14.2bn of R&D, 197 projects and 20 NMEs in late stages, with 9 of the 20 NMEs targeted for 2030 already delivered {AR|pp. 10, 28, 30}. The AR says the success of its R&D is crucial over the longer term {AR|p. 52}.
  3. Capacity to execute and fund (S4 and S5): more than 99% supply performance and operating cash flow of $14,575m support the $50bn US plan {AR|pp. 4, 10}, against net debt of $23,374m {AR|p. 61}.

External situation.

  1. Loss of exclusivity and IRA pricing for Farxiga in 2026 (T2 and T1): the nearest and most quantifiable event {AR|pp. 33, 56}. Calquence follows in 2027 {AR|p. 33}.
  2. US pricing and policy (T1, T3, O6): the US is 43% of revenue and is projected at about 50% by 2030, and an October 2025 agreement aligns US prices with other wealthy countries {AR|pp. 2, 4, 32}.
  3. Structural demand (O1, O2, O5): healthcare spending grows 7.1% a year, and the disease burden keeps rising {AR|pp. 6, 7, 13, 17}. This is the counterweight to the two threats above.

Link between the two. Launching new medicines faster than old ones lose exclusivity is the critical success factor. Today one established product, Brilinta, has already lost 38% of its sales, and the largest, Farxiga, is about to lose US exclusivity {AR|pp. 33, 56}. The market grows in value, and the value of each product is increasingly limited in time and in price.

Growth through innovation, with accelerated renewal of the portfolio, geographic balance and secure supply. It combines the SO (grow) and ST (defend) quadrants:

  1. Concentrate R&D investment on the therapy areas where the AR claims or seeks leadership (oncology, cardiovascular, renal and metabolic, respiratory and immunology, and rare diseases), using AI to cut development time. Basis: S1, S2, S6 → O1, O4, O5. It also answers W4 through late-stage programmes such as baxdrostat {AR|p. 17}.

  3. Reinforce growth in emerging markets (revenue of $15.3bn, +12%; +19% outside China {AR|pp. 4, 33}) and keep local manufacturing where governments encourage it, as in the US, China and Singapore {AR|pp. 4, 5, 32, 37}. Basis: S3 → O3, O6, T3. It sits in tension with the US reaching about 50% of revenue by 2030 {AR|p. 4}.
  4. Demonstrate the economic value of the medicines to payers, with tiered pricing and outcome-based agreements. Basis: answer to T1.
  5. Secure supply and deliver the climate plan: keep supply performance above 99% {AR|p. 4}, expand capacity {AR|p. 33} and close the gap to the 98% Scope 1 and 2 target {AR|p. 42}. Basis: S4 → T6, W5.

W2 (US dependence) and W6 (pipeline setbacks) are recognised as risks and have no dedicated action. T4 and T5 are outside the Group's control, and the strategy answers them indirectly by renewing the portfolio.
